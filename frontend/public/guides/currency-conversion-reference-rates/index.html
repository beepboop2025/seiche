How to convert currencies with dated reference rates and fees

By Seiche · Published and source-checked 4 October 2026 · Educational research

To convert a currency amount, establish which currency the rate prices, multiply or divide in the correct direction, and apply explicitly stated fees. For a reference-rate valuation, keep the provider and observation date with the result. A central-bank reference is useful context; it is not an offer to exchange money at that price.

Does USD/INR mean multiply or divide?

In this guide, USD/INR means INR per one USD. If a hypothetical reference is 90 INR per USD, 1,000 USD has a reference value of 90,000 INR. Converting 90,000 INR back to USD divides by 90. The inverse rate is 1/90 USD per INR, not another independent observation.

Always read the source's unit labels. The Federal Reserve H.10 release includes different quotation conventions for different currencies. A label copied without its unit can reverse the meaning of an appreciation or depreciation.

How do you calculate a same-date cross rate?

If a provider reports both USD per EUR and INR per EUR for the same date, divide INR-per-EUR by USD-per-EUR to obtain INR per USD. For hypothetical inputs of 99 INR per EUR and 1.10 USD per EUR, the cross is 90 INR per USD. Both legs must come from the same provider, compatible fixing convention and observation date.

The ECB's euro reference-rate page explains the publication and information-only purpose of its reference rates. A calculated cross remains a derived reference. Combining yesterday's leg with today's leg silently creates a different object.

Weekends, local holidays and publication timing can explain different last-observation dates. Keep an older observation labelled with its own date. A fresh download of an old record does not create a fresh FX observation.

Worked example: compare the amount after stated fees

Hypothetical example, not a current quote. Convert 1,000 USD at 90 INR per USD. Gross reference value is 90,000 INR. A 100-basis-point charge is 1%, or 900 INR; an additional fixed receiving-currency charge of 10 INR leaves 89,090 INR.

The calculation is net = amount × rate − amount × rate × fee_bps / 10,000 − fixed_receiving_currency_fee. This formula assumes the percentage charge applies to the gross converted amount. A provider may charge differently or embed a spread in its quoted rate, so compare the actual charging terms before combining fees.

Do not count an embedded spread twice. Also keep transfer charges, taxes, correspondent deductions, settlement delays and minimum charges separate if they are not included. The result is only as complete as the stated inputs.

Use the Seiche FX workbench

Open the FX workbench, choose an official provider and currency pair, and inspect the observation date, source records and history. The amount converter calculates a reference valuation after your entered fees. Results and the reference identity can be downloaded together; editing the pair or inputs clears the previous result.

For USD, INR and AED corridor questions, the GIFT City desk keeps ECB and CBUAE references separately identified. For a physical gold sale, use Undertow's gold workbench with an actual separately obtained bid and FX quote. Reference values do not establish available cash or market access.
