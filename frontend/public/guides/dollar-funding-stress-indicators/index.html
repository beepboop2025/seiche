How to read dollar-funding stress: SOFR, reserves and the calendar

By Seiche · Published and source-checked 4 October 2026 · Educational research

Read dollar-funding stress through several separate observations: overnight borrowing rates relative to policy rates, reserve conditions, repo activity and upcoming cash demands. Align their dates before drawing a conclusion. One elevated rate, a lower reserve balance or a large settlement is a reason to investigate, not by itself proof of a system-wide funding shortage.

What do SOFR and IORB measure?

SOFR is the New York Fed's broad measure of overnight cash borrowing costs against Treasury collateral. IORB is the Federal Reserve's administered interest rate on eligible reserve balances. They describe different activities, so their difference needs interpretation rather than a universal good-or-bad threshold.

Hypothetical arithmetic, not current rates: if SOFR is 4.91% and the IORB rate applicable to that observation date is 4.90%, the spread is (4.91 − 4.90) × 100 = +1 basis point. A rate in percentage points must be multiplied by 100 to express its difference in basis points. Using the previous policy rate across a rate change would give a misleading result.

Also distinguish the overnight SOFR observation from a compounded SOFR average and from a forward-looking term rate. Their horizons and construction differ. The publication time of an overnight reference rate is not the time at which the underlying borrowing occurred.

Which evidence belongs in a funding review?

Separate the price, quantity and timing questions
EvidenceQuestionInterpretation limit
SOFR, TGCR and EFFR relative to policy ratesIs overnight funding becoming more expensive?Different markets, participants and collateral; one spread is not a complete diagnosis.
Reserve balances and their distributionHow much cash is held in the banking system, and where?An aggregate does not reveal every institution's usable liquidity.
Repo and central-bank facility activityWhere is cash being supplied or demanded?Read eligibility, transaction date and operation terms before explaining usage.
Treasury cash, tax dates and settlementsWhen might payment and financing needs change?A prospective demand is not an observed funding disruption.
Market functioningAre funding pressures accompanied by impaired trading conditions?A coincident market move does not establish its cause.

The New York Fed's discussion of reserve conditions explains why several prices and quantities matter, and why the reserve level consistent with ample conditions cannot be identified with certainty from a single fixed number. Its review of liquidity and funding conditions also distinguishes impaired liquidity from the ability of a market to continue functioning.

How do you distinguish a calendar warning from realised stress?

Keep two statements separate: “a large financing need is approaching” and “funding is already difficult.” The first is a scenario to monitor. The second needs contemporaneous evidence. A settlement date can guide when to look more closely without establishing that a cash shortage will occur.

As an analyst's workflow, record the expected event, the evidence that would confirm pressure and the evidence that would weaken that interpretation. For example, a dated cash-demand hypothesis should be revisited alongside realised overnight rates and available operation data. Do not promote the hypothesis to an observed event merely because the calendar date arrived.

If the rate observation is daily but another series is weekly, display both native observation dates. Downloading both files today does not make them same-day evidence. Missing, delayed and revised observations should remain visible in the written conclusion.

A repeatable five-step funding review

  1. Resolve the exact series. Identify market, maturity, units, source and publication convention.
  2. Align comparisons. Pair each overnight rate with the policy rate effective on the observation date; keep mismatched clocks explicit.
  3. Inspect several channels. Look at prices, quantities, facilities and scheduled demands without treating a missing channel as calm.
  4. State the inference. Distinguish a source observation, a calculated spread, a model classification and an analyst hypothesis.
  5. Preserve a review record. Keep the source URLs, observation dates, retrieval time and the conditions that would change the conclusion.
