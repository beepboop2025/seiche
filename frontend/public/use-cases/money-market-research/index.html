How to research a money market

Begin with the local benchmark and its native clock, test it against the relevant system-cash ledger, then route the exposure and exit questions to the products built for them.

Use Seiche to establish the funding state. Retrieve the local benchmark only where rights permit, attach its source and as-of time, and compare like with like inside that market's own history. Then examine reserves, facilities, Treasury cash, volumes and distributions. A market pack's registration or discovery record is not itself an observation.

Split the downstream question. LiquiLens addresses which institution's balance sheet may be exposed; Undertow addresses whether depth and exit capacity are deteriorating. Neither handoff turns correlation into causality.

Clock discipline

Do not make weekly evidence daily

A synthesis is only as current as the component used for that claim.

observation time
When the benchmark or stock applies economically.
publication time
When the original publisher made that observation available.
knowledge time
When Seiche captured or learned the record; this is not the observation date.
retrieval time
When the researcher fetched the API response; preserve it for reproducibility.
Frequently asked questions

Money-market scope, plainly

These visible answers match the structured FAQ.

Which product should I use for money-market research?

Start with Seiche for system funding, repo, reserves, Treasury cash and local benchmark context. Use LiquiLens only when the question moves to a named financial institution, and Undertow when it moves to market depth, crowding or position-sized exit liquidity.

Does a missing money-market observation mean the market is calm?

No. A missing, stale, restricted or ineligible observation is an evidence gap. Seiche keeps that state visible and does not replace it with zero, a policy rate or a calm score.

Can Seiche recommend a money-market trade?

No. Seiche publishes research evidence and derived context. It does not provide investment advice, recommend instruments, quote executable prices or guarantee that a funding condition will produce a market outcome.
