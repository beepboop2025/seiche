A newly censor-targeted-terms reading is the fresh, conspicuous change on Seiche’s board: 97.0 terms, flagged at 4.6 sigma against its own history. It is not evidence that dollar funding is tight. The series is context-only, does not enter the composite, and is not a model feature.

The funding case is narrower. Seiche’s 44.9 EROSION reading is driven by a calendar path in which Treasury settlement and a rebuilt Treasury General Account coincide with reserves already below a fitted demand-curve kink. That could reduce immediately deployable cash and intermediary balance-sheet capacity when settlement demand arrives. It has not yet done so in the observable price of overnight cash.

The conclusion is a vulnerability watch, not a scarcity call: the calendar is carrying the strain thesis; the secured funding market still reads as abundant.

## The mechanism or historical setup

Treasury cash-balance rebuilding and Treasury security settlement can drain reserves. A funding event does not follow automatically. The relevant question is whether the remaining cash and balance sheet are held by institutions able to intermediate the flow when it arrives. Aggregate reserves can coexist with a local shortage of usable capacity.

The September 2019 repo spike is a bounded historical guide. Corporate tax payments and Treasury settlement needs converged on a system with less immediately deployable cash than calm headline conditions suggested. Secured overnight rates moved before the broader risk-asset narrative signalled a crisis. This is background, not a forecast: its relevance is the transmission mechanism, not a claim of recurrence.

Seiche’s current construction makes that mechanism explicit. Its dated-reserve-path component is saturated at 100.0 and contributes 11.0 points to the composite. The board’s tell is 46.7: plumbing sits at the 65th percentile of its own history while market stress is at the 18th percentile, a gap of 47 percentile points.

## Quantified evidence with as-of context

The observed ledger supplies the first link. Reserves fell $30.4B in the week through 2026-09-02 to $2,895B. The Treasury General Account rebuilt $17.2B, the residual absorbed $14.5B, and the Federal Reserve balance sheet added $6.3B. Six ledger legs sum to the reserve change to the dollar; $37.5B of the balance sheet, 0.56% of assets, remains in the unnamed residual. Seiche’s underlying presentation is available in its [data overview](https://seiche.info/data/overview.json), with construction described in the [methodology](https://seiche.info/methodology).

A fitted reserve-demand curve places reserves $809B below a $3,703.4B kink as of 2026-09-02, with an R-squared of 0.617. This is structural evidence, not an observed funding price. Its central contradiction is that SOFR was 0.8bp below IORB as of 2026-09-02. In the latest headline observations, SOFR was 3.66% and EFFR 3.63% as of 2026-09-03; IORB was 3.65% as of 2026-09-08.

The calendar provides the proposed forcing. A $246B Treasury auction settlement is scheduled for 2026-09-10. Seiche’s reserve-path scenario places reserves at $2,927.5B on that date, with a worst-case estimate of $2,874.8B. A $119B settlement follows on 2026-09-15, with a $2,836.9B worst-case estimate; 2026-09-16 is separately marked as a calendar-pressure date, with a $2,809.9B worst-case estimate. These are modelled paths, not observed outcomes.

A separate, slower-moving signal comes from official flows. Foreign officials cut Fed custody Treasuries by $45.7B over 13 weeks through 2026-09-02 while foreign RRP parking rose by $33.5B. The combined official footprint still fell $12.2B to $2,958B. Seiche calculates that 27% of the custody decline left the official sector rather than rotating within it.

## The strongest counter-case

The counter-case defeats any claim that funding is already tight. SOFR below IORB is a direct current-abundance signal. The Standing Repo Facility accepted $0.00B on 2026-09-04, so the backstop was not being tested in size. Market stress stood at only the 18th percentile of its own history as of 2026-09-03.

Those observations matter more than a modelled reserve path for a present-tense scarcity claim. A fitted kink can be wrong or incomplete; reserves can be below it without producing a shortage if cash remains available to the institutions that need it. The quiet secured-rate tape is evidence, not an objection to be explained away.

Nor should the event models be translated into literal odds. Seiche’s pooled five-business-day event read is 6.4%, but its validation note says probability levels do not beat climatology and are for ranking and alerting rather than literal probabilities. The selected ensemble does not outrank the rule-based index, and the analog framework says its analogs do not beat climatology on this sample.

## A falsifiable next test

The test is observable at the settlement dates. If the calendar mechanism is constraining usable funding capacity, the $246B settlement on 2026-09-10 and the $119B settlement on 2026-09-15 should be accompanied by deterioration in the SOFR-IORB relationship or meaningful Standing Repo Facility use. Lower modelled reserves alone do not validate the thesis.

The failure condition is equally clear. If SOFR remains below IORB and Standing Repo Facility acceptance stays near $0.00B through those dates, the case that calendar forcing is tightening usable dollar-funding capacity should weaken materially.

The next dated monitor is quarter-end on 2026-09-30. Seiche publishes a 5.2bp forecast with a 1.3bp to 9.9bp band and severity of 2. That is a monitor, not a prediction.

## Follow the pressure chain

Follow the chain from reserve drainage and settlement dates to secured overnight pricing and facility use. Only if those links move should a domestic plumbing concern be treated as a broader dollar-capacity event.

Offshore dollar moorings are a later corroboration test, not proof of domestic funding conditions. USDT deviation was -1.0bp as of 2026-09-06, while stablecoin supply was $310.6B, up 1.45% over 30 days. That calm reading does not rule out a reserve-distribution problem; it sets a higher bar for claims of generalized dollar stress.

Seiche covers system dollar-funding capacity. [LiquiLens](https://liquilens.in/) covers institution and lender balance-sheet risk. [Undertow](https://liquilens-undertow.com/) covers market liquidity, crowding, and exit capacity. These are distinct layers, and none substitutes for confirmation in secured funding prices.
