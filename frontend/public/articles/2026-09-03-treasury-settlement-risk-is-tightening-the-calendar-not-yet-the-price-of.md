The material new change is in rates rather than overnight funding. The [2-year Treasury constant-maturity yield](https://api.seiche.info/api/series/index.json) rose 0.05 percentage point to 4.39% on its latest print, a 3.2-sigma level against its own history. Seiche’s funding board reads 45, EROSION, with the dated reserve path contributing 11.0 points and plumbing leading market pricing by +38 percentile points.

The cautious conclusion is that dollar-funding capacity is becoming more vulnerable through the calendar channel, not that it is already scarce. Treasury settlement, tax timing and quarter-end can require balance-sheet and reserve capacity on specific dates. But the current price of overnight cash and use of the official backstop do not confirm an active shortage.

## The mechanism or historical setup

The mechanism is a collision between a reserve position and dated cash demands. Reserves fell $10.4B in the week to $2,925B. The ledger attributes a $17.9B release from the foreign RRP pool, a $14.9B absorption by the residual, and a $14.8B contraction in the Federal Reserve balance sheet. Six legs sum to the reserve change, while $22.9B of the balance sheet, 0.34% of assets, remains in an unnamed residual.

That accounting is not a stress print. It shows the flows against which future settlement demands will land. Seiche’s fitted reserve-demand curve places reserves $779B below its estimated kink. The fit has an R-squared of 0.62, so the kink is a modelled structural reference rather than an observed hard threshold. Its relevance is conditional: settlement or tax flows could make marginal funding more sensitive if they arrive when reserves are already below that estimate.

The first dated demand is a $175B Treasury auction settlement on September 8. Seiche’s reserve path has a worst-case reserve estimate of $2,950.2B on that date. Further calendar-pressure dates fall on September 15 and September 16, with worst-case estimates of $2,912.4B and $2,879.0B. The claim is not that an auction settlement mechanically creates a funding event. It is that these dates test whether the system can absorb cash demands without a change in funding prices or recourse to the backstop.

## Quantified evidence with as-of context

The [daily dispatch](https://seiche.info/dispatches/2026-09-03-daily) identifies the dated reserve path as the largest board contribution: the weather component is saturated at 100.0 and contributes 11.0 points. The kink component scores 56.4 and contributes 7.3 points. The 44.6 composite is a Seiche derivation, not an observed market price. Its 100.0% coverage means designated inputs are live; it does not establish that a funding event will occur.

Foreign-official data add context but not a withdrawal signal. Foreign officials cut Fed custody Treasuries by $64.4B over 13 weeks while foreign RRP parking rose $56.4B, leaving an official-sector footprint of $2,971B. Seiche characterizes this as a parking rotation rather than the official bid leaving.

The directly observed funding prices remain calm. SOFR was 3.65% and EFFR 3.63% as of September 2; IORB was 3.65% as of September 3. In the reserve-demand assessment, SOFR was 0.9bp below IORB as of August 26. Neither reading is a scarcity signal.

Risk markets have not supplied broad confirmation. The VIX was 15.2 and the high-yield option-adjusted spread was 2.66% as of September 2. Market stress sat at the 22nd percentile of its history, against a plumbing percentile of 60. That divergence is the central fact: the calendar and structural measures are ahead of market-price evidence.

The forward read is similarly restrained. The pooled five-business-day event reading is 6.1%. A separate machine-learning measure was 0.4% and did not outrank the rule-based index. The disagreement is a reason for guarded confidence, not a basis to select the higher estimate.

## The strongest counter-case

The counter-case can defeat the strain thesis: capacity may simply be ample enough to absorb the calendar. SOFR is at IORB on the latest board readings, and the earlier SOFR discount to IORB indicates abundance rather than scarcity. The Standing Repo Facility accepted $0.00B as of September 3. The official backstop is not being tested in size.

The reserve-kink signal is also contingent on a fitted relationship with an R-squared of 0.62. Being below an estimated kink does not itself demonstrate tight funding. Nor do foreign custody and foreign RRP changes demonstrate a retreat by official buyers; Seiche reads them as a parking rotation.

The published September 30 quarter-end forecast is 5.2bp, with a 1.3bp to 9.9bp band and severity 2. A separate model estimate is 13.1bp, but Seiche publishes the naive forecast rather than the larger figure. The curve model says it beats climatology out of sample, while the analogs do not beat climatology on their sample. If the calendar dates pass with SOFR at or below IORB, no meaningful Standing Repo Facility use, and no market-stress confirmation, then the appropriate conclusion is not merely that stress was avoided. It is that the calendar signal did not establish tightening dollar-funding capacity.

## A falsifiable next test

September 8 is the first observable test. The strain case gains credibility only if the $175B settlement coincides with deterioration in the price or use of funding capacity: SOFR moves above IORB, Standing Repo Facility take-up appears, or market stress rises as the gap between plumbing and market pricing closes.

The thesis fails its near-term test if the settlement and the September 15 and September 16 pressure dates pass without those changes—particularly if SOFR remains at or below IORB and the facility remains at $0.00B. September 30 provides a further benchmark: a quiet quarter-end within the published 1.3bp to 9.9bp band would reinforce the counter-case that the calendar was absorbable.

## Follow the pressure chain

The relevant chain runs from reserve availability to dealer intermediation, secured dollar funding and offshore-dollar liquidity. It should not be read in reverse: an offshore marker can register transmission but cannot establish the underlying cause.

The offshore-dollar context does not currently show a break. USDT’s peg deviation was -1.5bp as of September 3. Stablecoin supply totaled $309.2B and rose 1.17% over 30 days. These measures are context only and do not enter the Seiche composite or a model feature.

A separate issue is market liquidity and exit capacity if funding pressure does emerge. Seiche’s warehouse measure showed $407.5B of total net positioning at the 95th percentile as of August 19, with 35.9% in the long end. That does not predict liquidation; it distinguishes system funding capacity from crowding and the capacity to exit positions.
