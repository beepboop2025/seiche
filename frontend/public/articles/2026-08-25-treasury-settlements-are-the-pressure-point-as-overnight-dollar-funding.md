The thesis is narrow. Dollar-funding capacity may be tightening through the timing and distribution of reserve drains, not through a broad shortage already visible in overnight rates. It must therefore be confirmed in transactions: a structural warning is insufficient on its own.

## The mechanism or historical setup

Auction settlements move cash into Treasury accounts and can reduce bank reserve balances. That does not mechanically create scarcity. Balances can be redistributed and private intermediaries can meet funding demand. The relevant constraint emerges only when banks or dealers are less willing or able to perform that redistribution at prevailing rates.

The calendar is the dominant driver in Seiche’s construction. Its dated reserve-path component scores 100.0 and contributes 11.0 points to the composite. The board identifies a $95B auction settlement on 2026-08-27 while reserves sit below an estimated reserve-demand kink, followed by a $183B settlement on 2026-08-31. These are scheduled forcing events, not observed funding stress.

The desk’s historical guide points to the December 2025 year-end squeeze, when reporting incentives reduced willingness to intermediate while balance-sheet demand was seasonally high. Standing Repo Facility use made the constraint observable rather than inferred from a single rate. That comparison is context only: the engine fields are construction point-in-time comparisons, not publication-vintage backtests or forecasts.

## The strongest counter-case

The counter-case can defeat the thesis: actual overnight secured funding has not confirmed a scarcity condition. The editorial evidence records SOFR at 0.9 basis points below IORB as of 2026-08-19. The board headline shows SOFR and IORB both at 3.65% on their respective latest observations. Neither reading shows secured funding trading above the rate paid on reserve balances.

The official backstop is also untested in size. Standing Repo Facility accepted volume was $0.00B as of 2026-08-24. Market stress was at the 28th percentile of its own history as of 2026-08-21.

This evidence matters more than the estimated kink if it persists through settlement. The kink may be imprecise or institutionally uneven, and intermediaries may absorb reserve redistribution without a disruptive move in secured funding. In that outcome, the calendar identifies a potential drain, not tightening dollar-funding capacity.

## A falsifiable next test

The observable test is the settlement window around the $95B settlement on 2026-08-27 and the $183B settlement on 2026-08-31. Seiche’s worst-case reserve paths are $2,896.7B and $2,881.2B. They are scenarios, not forecasts.

The thesis gains support if SOFR moves persistently above IORB during that window or if Standing Repo Facility acceptance becomes meaningful. It is weakened if SOFR remains at or below IORB and the facility remains unused despite the scheduled drain. That test requires the price and use of funding capacity to validate the calendar signal.

## Follow the pressure chain

Follow the chain from Treasury settlement to reserve balances, then to banks’ and dealers’ willingness to intermediate, then to SOFR relative to IORB and finally to backstop use. The move in the 2-year Treasury yield is a fresh market signal, but it is not the funding mechanism.

The board’s offshore-dollar context does not currently show a break: USDT’s peg deviation was -2.0 basis points as of 2026-08-25. That is context for transmission risk, not evidence that a domestic funding constraint has already transmitted offshore.
