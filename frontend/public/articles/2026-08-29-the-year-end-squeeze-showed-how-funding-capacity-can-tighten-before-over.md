The episode is the December 2025 year-end squeeze, when Standing Repo Facility use reached a $74.6B record. Its lesson is narrower than a prediction. Funding can tighten through a loss of private intermediation capacity before an overnight benchmark alone provides an unambiguous scarcity signal.

## The mechanism or historical setup

The desk’s bounded guide describes the December 2025 setup as a collision between year-end reporting incentives and seasonally high balance-sheet demand. Reporting incentives reduced the willingness to intermediate just as demand for balance-sheet capacity was high. Heavy Standing Repo Facility take-up made that constraint observable rather than inferred from a single rate.

The mechanism is a capacity chain. If marginal balance-sheet deployment becomes less attractive, dealers and banks may intermediate less financing demand. The private channel that redistributes cash, collateral and secured funding narrows. Demand that private balance sheets do not absorb can then appear at the public backstop.

That does not mean aggregate cash is necessarily scarce. It means the ability and willingness to place balance sheets between cash and collateral can be the binding constraint. The backstop has a dual interpretation: its use can reveal constrained private capacity, while its availability can limit the chance that the constraint becomes a disorderly shortage.

## Follow the pressure chain

Follow the historical chain from reporting incentives to lower balance-sheet willingness, from lower willingness to reduced private intermediation, and from reduced intermediation to backstop demand. December 2025 showed why a funding-capacity problem can become visible at the backstop before a headline overnight rate fully describes it.

Then follow the current chain in reverse. SOFR below IORB, $0.00B of accepted Standing Repo Facility usage and market stress at the 20th percentile say that private confirmation had not arrived. The guarded conclusion is therefore precise: the calendar was carrying the strain call; the price of overnight cash still said abundance.
