The material change is not the small ON RRP level. It is Seiche’s dated reserve path, the largest contributor to an EROSION board reading. The mechanism is straightforward but unconfirmed: scheduled Treasury settlement and other calendar demands could draw on reserves that Seiche estimates are below its fitted reserve-demand kink.

Overnight prices and official repo operations presently argue against scarcity. That makes this a calendar warning, not a funding event. The proposition can fail: if the scheduled settlement clears while SOFR remains below IORB and the Standing Repo Facility remains unused, the market evidence defeats the reserve-path thesis for this window.

## The mechanism or historical setup

Reserves fell $10.4B in the week through August 26 to $2,925B. Seiche’s ledger attributes that weekly movement across six legs that sum to the reserve change: foreign RRP balances released $17.9B, the residual absorbed $14.9B, and the Federal Reserve balance sheet shrank $14.8B. The ledger leaves $22.9B, or 0.34% of assets, in an unnamed residual. It identifies an accounting pressure point without fully identifying its source.

Seiche says reserves were $779B below its fitted reserve-demand kink as of August 26. The fitted kink is a model construction, not a traded threshold, and its fit has an R-squared of 0.62. The conditional mechanism is that reserve demand could steepen below that estimate, allowing a dated reserve drain to pressure the price or availability of balance-sheet capacity.

The first scheduled test is September 8, when $247B of Treasury auction settlement is due. Seiche’s reserve-path scenarios place reserves at $2,962.3B in its forecast and $2,901.2B in its worst case. September 15 and September 16 are further calendar-pressure dates, with worst-case scenarios of $2,863.1B and $2,829.3B. These are scenarios, not observations.

The March 2023 regional-bank run is a bounded historical caution rather than a forecast. Its lesson is that system liquidity and institution liquidity can diverge: official facilities can stabilize aggregate plumbing while individual balance sheets remain impaired. The comparison is desk background; the engine fields are construction-point-in-time comparisons, not publication-vintage backtests or forecasts.

## The strongest counter-case

The counter-case can defeat the thesis because it relies on current funding prices and actual facility use rather than reserve-path scenarios. As of August 26, SOFR was 0.9bp below IORB, an abundance signal rather than a scarcity print. The Standing Repo Facility accepted $0.00B on September 2, so the formal backstop was not being tested in size. Market stress stood at the 29th percentile of its own history as of September 1.

This is more than a claim that markets are complacent. It is a claim that reserves, intermediation capacity and backstop credibility are sufficient for the calendar to clear without a damaging repricing. Seiche’s published September 30 quarter-end forecast is 5.2bp, with a 1.3bp to 10.0bp band and severity of 2 on a scale from 1 calm to 5 extreme. A 13.1bp model estimate exists, but Seiche publishes the 5.2bp naive forecast.

If SOFR remains below IORB after the dated pressure window and accepted SRF take-up remains $0.00B, the observable evidence supports the abundance case over the reserve-path warning.

## A falsifiable next test

September 8 is the first test. Compare reported SOFR with IORB on aligned observations after the $247B settlement, and record accepted SRF take-up. The calendar-strain thesis gains support only if SOFR deteriorates to or above IORB and accepted SRF use rises above $0.00B.

The thesis fails for this window if the settlement clears while SOFR remains below IORB and accepted SRF take-up remains $0.00B. September 15, September 16 and September 30 provide successive dated retests. The ON RRP print alone cannot pass this test.

## Follow the pressure chain

Follow the sequence rather than a single gauge: Treasury settlement and reserve changes; secured overnight pricing relative to IORB; use of official repo backstops; then offshore-dollar conditions. Seiche’s offshore-dollar anchor showed a USDT peg deviation of -6.0bp as of September 2, while stablecoin supply was $309B and up 1.15% over 30 days. Those observations do not independently establish a dollar-funding break.
