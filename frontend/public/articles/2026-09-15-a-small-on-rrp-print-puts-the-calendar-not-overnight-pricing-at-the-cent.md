ON RRP take-up stood at **$1.42B** as of **2026-09-14**, flagged by Seiche for a **2.92**-sigma daily change. That is the material new board change. It is not, however, a large cash flow: the level is a minute fraction of its referee-history peak.

The more consequential question is whether dollar-funding capacity is becoming more vulnerable through the calendar. Seiche’s guarded answer is that projected reserve pressure is building at dated points while the current price of overnight cash still indicates abundance. This is a vulnerability thesis, not a claim that a broad funding shortage is already under way.

Seiche’s composite reads **44.1**, or **EROSION**, and its plumbing measure leads market pricing by **19.1 percentile points** as of **2026-09-14**. The composite is a Seiche derivation, not an observed market price. Its largest contribution is the dated reserve path, at **11.0** points. The central question is therefore testable: do calendar drains translate into cash-price pressure or backstop use?

## The mechanism or historical setup

The proposed mechanism begins with reserves relative to Seiche’s fitted reserve-demand curve. Reserve balances were **$2,991.31B** as of **2026-09-09**, versus an estimated kink of **$3,703.2B**. The fit has an R-squared of **0.618**, so the kink is an imperfect structural guide, not a hard threshold.

Below-kink reserves matter only if dated flows further narrow the capacity to absorb settlement. Seiche identifies pressure on **2026-09-15** and **2026-09-16**, followed by a **$247B** Treasury auction settlement on **2026-09-22**. Its worst-case reserve path is **$2,936.4B**, **$2,901.5B**, and **$2,873.2B** on those dates. For **2026-09-30**, the modeled worst case is **$2,834.8B**.

The causal claim should remain narrow. Calendar flows can alter reserve distribution at the same time as settlement demand rises. That may tighten marginal funding or intermediation capacity without implying that aggregate reserves have disappeared. The ON RRP observation is relevant as a small change in overnight-facility use; it does not show that the mechanism has begun to bind.

A historical comparison does not establish a forecast. Seiche’s closest context analog is the March **2020** dash-for-cash, with similarity of **0.688** and a **6**-day lead. The useful lesson is only that aggregate liquidity and the ability to distribute it can diverge. Similarity is not recurrence.

## Quantified evidence with as-of context

The latest ledger qualifies any simple tightening story. Reserves **rose $96.8B** in the week through **2026-09-09**. Seiche attributes the change to an **$84.6B** TGA spend-down, an **$8.0B** release from the foreign RRP pool, and a **$6.2B** residual release. The six legs sum to the reserve change to the dollar. Yet **$31.3B**, or **0.46%** of assets, remains in an unnamed residual, limiting precision about the impulse.

Official-sector flows point to a change in parking rather than an exit. Over **13** weeks through **2026-09-09**, foreign officials reduced Fed custody Treasuries by **$43.3B** while foreign RRP parking rose **$32.3B**. Seiche describes this as a parking rotation; the official footprint is **$2,939B**.

The divergence is between structural and market signals. Seiche places plumbing at the **55th** percentile and market stress at the **36th** percentile. Its pooled five-business-day event read is **5.7%**, but the published ensemble does not beat climatology on probability levels. That read is useful for alerting, not as literal odds.

For quarter-end, Seiche publishes a **5.2bp** forecast with a **1.1bp** to **9.6bp** band, rather than its **11.5bp** model estimate. The desk is therefore explicitly declining to turn its stronger structural estimate into the published central call.

## The strongest counter-case

The direct evidence favors abundance in overnight cash. SOFR was **3.62%** as of **2026-09-14**, while IORB was **3.65%** as of **2026-09-15**. In Seiche’s reserve-demand framing, SOFR was **0.4bp** below IORB as of **2026-09-09**. That is an abundance signal, not a scarcity print.

The repo backstop is also untested in size. Standing Repo Facility accepted take-up was **$0.0B** as of **2026-09-15**. If calendar pressure were already generating broad dollar-funding tightness, cash pricing above its policy anchor or meaningful facility use would be more compelling evidence than a small ON RRP print.

Broader markets have not confirmed the stress thesis. The VIX was **17.1** and high-yield option-adjusted spread was **2.71%**, both as of **2026-09-14**. Seiche’s market-stress measure was at the **36th** percentile. Its dynamical readings describe dominant modes as decaying and the current state as locally contracting. The available market evidence says disturbances have been damping rather than amplifying.

The ON RRP change belongs in this counter-case as well. A **$1.42B** level can be unusual relative to a near-zero base and still be economically small. A detection flag is not proof of a material liquidity drain.

## A falsifiable next test

The thesis should be judged by transmission, not by a lower modeled reserve number alone. The key date is **2026-09-22**, when the **$247B** settlement coincides with Seiche’s **$2,873.2B** worst-case reserve estimate. Quarter-end on **2026-09-30** pairs a **$19.0B** settlement with the published **5.2bp** forecast.

The pressure thesis gains support only if observable plumbing confirms it: SOFR no longer trades below IORB, Standing Repo Facility use rises from **$0.0B**, or the quarter-end move challenges the published **1.1bp** to **9.6bp** band. If those developments do not occur, the counter-case wins: reserves can remain below a fitted kink without producing an observable shortage of overnight dollar-funding capacity.

## Follow the pressure chain

The chain runs from calendar pressure and reserve distribution to observable money-market behavior, then—only if the strain persists—to broader market pricing. Seiche’s warehouse measure shows **$419.4B** of total net positioning, at the **95th** percentile, with **34.5%** in the long end as of **2026-09-02**. That may make dated financing conditions more consequential, but it is not evidence of a current funding break.

The next observable links are SOFR relative to IORB, ON RRP activity, and Standing Repo Facility use. The offshore-dollar cross-check is also not confirming strain: USDT’s peg deviation was **-5.6bp** as of **2026-09-15**; stablecoin supply was **$310.3B**, up **1.36%** over the prior **30** days.

The conclusion is narrower than a risk call. Dollar-funding capacity is being tested through dated mechanics, while overnight pricing still says cash is abundant. The test is whether that calendar pressure crosses into observable funding behavior.
