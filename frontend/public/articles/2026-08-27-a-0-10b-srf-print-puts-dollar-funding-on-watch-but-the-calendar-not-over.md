The proposed transmission is straightforward: dated cash demands can alter reserve distribution; a less accommodating balance-sheet response could then appear in secured overnight pricing or SRF use. That final confirmation is absent so far.

Seiche’s composite is 45, or EROSION. The dated reserve path contributes 11.0 points, and plumbing leads market pricing by 32 percentile points. But the composite is a Seiche derivation, not an observed market price. The question is therefore not whether a funding event is already underway. It is whether calendar pressure will produce an observable funding response.

## The mechanism or historical setup

Reserves fell $10.4B on the week to $2,925B. The ledger records a $17.9B release from foreign RRP, a $14.9B absorption in the residual, and a $14.8B balance-sheet contraction across six legs that sum to the reserve change. It also leaves $22.9B of the balance sheet, or 0.34% of assets, in an unnamed residual. That residual limits the confidence warranted by any clean one-week narrative.

The pressure case comes from the calendar rather than a current dislocation in overnight cash. Seiche’s path flags month-end, a $308B auction settlement and a later calendar pressure date while reserves remain below the estimated kink. These are scenarios, not observed funding outcomes or claims that reserves must reach a particular level.

The mechanism is about marginal capacity. A system can show inexpensive aggregate overnight funding while balance-sheet willingness becomes more selective on particular dates. Confirmation would require that selectivity to become visible in the SOFR relationship to IORB or in meaningful SRF use.

## The strongest counter-case

The counter-case can defeat the thesis. It says Seiche is measuring a vulnerable-looking reserve path, not an actual shortage of dollar-funding capacity. SOFR below IORB is direct live evidence for that interpretation: secured overnight cash is not presently trading as though it is scarce.

The SRF print reinforces the point. $0.10B is statistically striking because the reference base is near zero, but it is only 0.14% of the monitor’s historical peak absolute reading. Broad demand for the backstop is not visible.

Market pricing is also calm. Market stress is at the 26th percentile of its own history. The VIX is 15.21 and the high-yield option-adjusted spread is 2.67%. The plumbing-versus-price divergence may be early warning, but it may instead show that slow-moving or modeled balance-sheet measures are sensitive to calendar mechanics that markets absorb without disruption.

The historical comparison cannot close that gap. Seiche’s selected analog is the March 2020 dash-for-cash, with 0.692 similarity and a 13-day construction-period lead. It is context, not a forecast. The desk’s bounded lesson is that funding monitors may reveal propagation into balance-sheet and market-liquidity strain, not foresee an outside shock.

## A falsifiable next test

The first test is month-end. Seiche publishes a naive forecast of a 3.5bp move, with a band from -0.5bp to 8.2bp and severity 2. Its model estimate is 10.3bp, but the published forecast remains the naive one.

The pressure thesis gains support only if the dated window is accompanied by a less favorable SOFR-to-IORB relationship and SRF acceptance that remains meaningfully above its near-zero pattern. It fails if SOFR remains below IORB and SRF usage returns toward that pattern through the $308B settlement. A reserve decline by itself does not pass the test: the proposed mechanism requires a price or backstop response.

## Follow the pressure chain

Follow the sequence rather than the headline print: dated cash demand affects reserve distribution; reserve distribution tests dealer and bank balance-sheet willingness; that willingness should appear in secured overnight pricing and, if needed, SRF usage. The evidence is strongest at the calendar-and-reserves link and weakest at the pricing and backstop links.

Offshore-dollar context is not currently signaling a break. The USDT peg deviation is 0.6bp, while tracked stablecoin supply is $309.2B, up 0.77% over 30 days. These are context markers, not evidence that digital markets are insulated from dollar plumbing.
