The lesson from the April 2025 tariff-shock basis unwind is narrower and more useful: dollar-funding capacity can tighten through the ability of dealers and leveraged holders to finance, hedge and exit correlated positions—not simply through the aggregate quantity of reserves. Overnight cash can still look abundant while that marginal capacity comes under strain.

## The mechanism or historical setup

The desk describes the April 2025 episode as a tariff-shock basis unwind. A policy shock changed risk and volatility faster than leveraged relative-value books could adjust. Treasury selling, basis pressure and dealer intermediation became a single transmission chain rather than separate market stories.

The mechanism runs from shock to volatility, then to leveraged-position adjustment. If many holders seek the same exit or hedge, flows concentrate in instruments that dealers must intermediate. As dealers absorb inventory and volatility, balance-sheet capacity becomes more valuable and potentially more selective. The relevant funding question is not only whether cash exists in aggregate. It is whether balance-sheet-intensive trades can remain funded while exits crowd together.

That does not mean a large position is automatically dangerous, or that every reserve drain becomes a market event. The historical lesson is conditional: strain can emerge when many holders require the same exit while dealers absorb the same volatility shock. An orderly overnight benchmark can coexist with impaired marginal capacity to warehouse risk.
