Why is SOFR above IORB?

It was not above IORB in the latest paired observation retained here: on 2 October 2026, SOFR was 3.88% and IORB was 3.90%, a −2 bp spread. When the spread does turn positive, it can reflect pressure on secured funding or on the intermediaries moving cash and collateral. The spread alone cannot tell you which mechanism caused it.

Why does the comparison have no automatic ceiling?

IORB is available to eligible institutions holding reserve balances. Repo markets connect a broader set of cash borrowers and lenders, with collateral, counterparty and balance-sheet constraints. Cash does not move between every participant without cost or limit.

Greater demand to finance Treasury holdings, changes in cash availability and limits on intermediary capacity can affect repo pricing. Month-end or quarter-end balance-sheet management can coincide with price pressure. Establishing a cause requires evidence about the particular date; a calendar label alone is not proof.

What would distinguish a brief move from broader pressure?

Check whether the positive spread persists across observations and whether the upper tail of repo rates, repo volumes and facility use move with it. Compare the Treasury General Account and reserve balances using their own publication dates. Do not turn a single one-day spike into a bank-failure claim.

What this evidence cannot establish

  • This is public research, not an executable funding quote, a forecast or a bank-solvency assessment.
  • A positive spread is a price signal. It does not, by itself, identify a cause or prove aggregate reserve scarcity.
  • The reviewed daily observations are older than the page review. Missing observations are not forward-filled to create a “today” number.
