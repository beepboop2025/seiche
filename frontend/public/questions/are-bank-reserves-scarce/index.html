US dollar funding research / evidence explained

Are bank reserves scarce or still ample?

The reserve total alone cannot establish whether reserves are scarce or ample. Seiche retained $2,948.09 billion of reserve balances for 30 September 2026, up $17.897 billion over one week. Its separate 2 October observation shows SOFR 2 bp below IORB and zero reported Standing Repo Facility use. Those readings do not support a simple claim of immediate system-wide scarcity, but they do not prove every institution has ample funding.

What does “ample” mean in practice?

In an ample-reserves operating framework, administered rates help control short-term interest rates without frequent changes in reserve supply. The relevant question is how funding prices respond as reserve supply and distribution change, not whether the aggregate stock exceeds one fixed number.

A large aggregate balance can coexist with friction at a particular bank or dealer. Payment timing, internal limits, access to facilities and the distribution of balances affect whether available cash reaches the participant that needs it.

How to read this particular example

The retained weekly reserve stock increased, while the later matched repo spread was negative and reported SRF use was zero. These are observations from different clocks. Together they provide context, not a contemporaneous causal test or a safety verdict.

A useful follow-up is to watch persistence: several observations of wider funding spreads, changing rate distributions and facility use carry a different research question from a one-day movement. Seiche keeps missing or aging evidence visible rather than treating it as reassurance.

What this evidence cannot establish

  • This is public research, not an executable funding quote, a forecast or a bank-solvency assessment.
  • A positive spread is a price signal. It does not, by itself, identify a cause or prove aggregate reserve scarcity.
  • The reviewed daily observations are older than the page review. Missing observations are not forward-filled to create a “today” number.
  • Weekly aggregates conceal distribution. These figures cannot identify which bank has a liquidity shortfall.
