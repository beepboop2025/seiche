What the FX layer can establish

The ledger supports sourced statements about the direction and scale of daily bilateral currency moves, broad dollar conditions and cross-market transmission into public funding and materials context.

Registered public series: the configured ledger contains a Federal Reserve H.10 reference-rate series for each listed currency plus the broad, advanced-foreign-economy and emerging-market trade-weighted dollar indexes. A series becomes observed only when the current API response contains a value, unit, source ID and as-of date.

Not established: the price currently available to trade, bid/ask spread, executable size, venue depth, forward carry, NDF curve, basis term structure, option-implied distribution or dealer positioning. Those require different instruments and, often, licensed data.

Method

Document first, normalize for comparison

FX series can point in opposite numerical directions solely because their quote convention differs. The page records each source convention, while the unified API normalizes every bilateral level to local-currency units per US dollar for comparison.

unit
The page records the published orientation, such as USD per EUR or JPY per USD. The unified API returns local per USD; a number without its stated unit is not a complete FX claim.
as_of
The source observation date. Weekends, holidays and non-publication days remain gaps rather than repeated synthetic observations.
retrieved_at
The capture clock used to diagnose delayed or stale upstream data.
normalization
An inversion used to produce local per USD is a Seiche transform. The original source value and convention remain available from the linked upstream publisher, not as a separate raw-value field in this API response.
Coverage frontier

The forex market beyond H.10

A reference-rate panel is useful macro evidence, but the global FX market also includes instruments and market microstructure that cannot be inferred from a daily fixing.

unavailable Intraday spot

No tick stream, minute bars, venue order book, dealer composite, bid/ask spread or executable size.

restricted Forwards and swaps

No complete forward-point, deliverable swap, NDF or cross-currency basis term structure on the public surface.

restricted Options

No full implied-volatility surface, delta risk reversals, butterflies, skew or expiry-by-strike chain.

structural Turnover and conventions

BIS surveys and settlement conventions may supply dated structural context, but dated structure is never presented as a live quote.

Frequently asked questions

Use the reference panel correctly

These visible answers are the same answers published in the page's structured FAQ data.

Which currencies does Seiche cover?

Seiche registers daily Federal Reserve H.10 reference series for the euro, British pound, Japanese yen, Australian dollar, Canadian dollar, Swiss franc, Chinese renminbi, Indian rupee, South Korean won, Mexican peso, Brazilian real, South African rand, New Zealand dollar, Danish krone, Hong Kong dollar, Malaysian ringgit, Norwegian krone, Swedish krona, Singapore dollar, Taiwan dollar, Thai baht and Sri Lankan rupee, plus three trade-weighted dollar indexes.

Are Seiche FX rates live trading prices?

No. When present, they are daily public reference observations, not bid/ask quotes, executable prices, order-book depth or intraday ticks.

Does Seiche cover FX forwards and options?

Not on the public evidence surface. Forward points, NDFs, cross-currency basis term structures, option volatility surfaces and risk reversals require additional eligible data and redistribution rights.

Citation protocol

Quote the orientation

An FX number without a pair convention can reverse the meaning of a move. Always retain the unit and source date.
